    Background

    Too many clothes, too little time to choose.

    Fast fashion, e-commerce and smartphone shopping have made clothing cheaper and easier to buy than ever. The result is crowded wardrobes, decision fatigue every morning, repeat purchases and a growing pile of textile waste. Studies suggest most people regularly wear only 20–30% of what they own, while the rest sits forgotten.

    Digital wardrobe apps exist, but most rely on manual tagging, show only flat images and are built to sell new items rather than help people use what they already have. Advances in computer vision, machine learning, knowledge graphs and 3D visualization now make a genuinely smart wardrobe possible.

    70–80%of clothes are rarely worn

    Research Problem

    Existing wardrobe apps make people do the work.

    Users tag every item by hand, get recommendations that ignore weather, events and personal style, and never see how an outfit will actually look on them. Wearza answers with four connected AI components.

    COMPONENT 01 · CNN + U²-Net

    AI Clothing Classification

    Removes the background from a phone photo, then a CNN predicts multiple attributes at once: type, colour, pattern and fabric. Google Trends analysis flags items you already own that are trending.

    • Background removal
    • Multi-attribute tagging
    • Trend alerts

    COMPONENT 02 · MongoDB + Analytics

    Smart Wardrobe Organization

    The data backbone. Tracks wear count and last-worn date, applies laundry logic so dirty items are skipped, indexes items for smart search and rotates forgotten garments back into use.

    • Wear count tracking
    • Laundry logic
    • Sustainability rotation

    COMPONENT 03 · Neo4j KG + ResNet

    Outfit Recommendation & Matchmaking

    A Fashion Knowledge Graph and ResNet visual features score outfits by compatibility, event, persona, micro-climate and sustainability, and explain every suggestion in plain words.

    • Explainable picks
    • Tropical micro-climate
    • Stable Diffusion studio

    COMPONENT 04 · Unity + AR Foundation

    Animated 3D Outfit Visualization

    A selfie and body measurements build a personal 3D avatar. Face texture is UV-mapped, clothes attach to the skeleton, and Mixamo animations show the outfit walking and turning in AR.

    • Face texture mapping
    • Body scaling
    • AR try-on

    The Impact

    Wear more of what you own. Buy less of what you don't need.

    By automating wardrobe management and explaining every recommendation, Wearza cuts daily decision fatigue, keeps forgotten clothes in rotation and encourages conscious, circular fashion habits.

    Literature Survey

    A

    Fashion image classification

    DeepFashion and DeepFashion2 established large benchmarks for clothing recognition, detection and attribute prediction. Residual networks (ResNet) and lightweight MobileNet models made accurate CNN classification practical, even in the browser. Segmentation models such as Mask R-CNN and U²-Net isolate garments from cluttered backgrounds, but most classifiers are still trained on clean studio images and predict a single attribute.

    B

    Digital & smart wardrobe systems

    Early smart closets used IoT hardware such as RFID tags, smart hangers and sensor-equipped cupboards to track inventory. These are expensive and offer little styling intelligence. App-based wardrobes remove the hardware but depend on manual tagging, and very few track wear frequency, laundry status or sustainability metrics.

    C

    Outfit recommendation & knowledge graphs

    Compatibility learning with Bi-LSTMs and graph networks (Han et al.), and Hierarchical Fashion Graph Networks for personalized outfits, improved matching. Knowledge graphs (Hogan et al.) and Graph Convolutional Networks add structure and explainability. Most systems still act as black boxes and use temperate four-season weather logic that fails in tropical climates.

    D

    Virtual try-on & 3D visualization

    Diffusion models (Ho et al.) and Stable Diffusion now generate realistic outfit imagery. Face landmark tools such as OpenCV and MediaPipe enable face texture extraction, and Unity with AR Foundation and Mixamo animation brings avatars into real environments. Existing wardrobe apps rarely connect this to the user's own clothes and body.

    Research Gap

    Manual tagging

    Users must enter type, colour and style by hand, so many abandon wardrobe apps.

    Clean-image bias

    Classifiers trained on studio datasets lose accuracy on real phone photos with clutter and shadows.

    No usage intelligence

    Few systems track wear count, laundry status or forgotten items to support sustainable rotation.

    Black-box, temperate logic

    Recommendations lack explanations and ignore humidity, indoor/outdoor micro-climates and cultural events.

    Static previews

    Outfits are shown as flat images, not on a personalized, animated avatar.

    Isolated modules

    Classification, organization, recommendation and visualization are researched separately, never as one platform.

    Problem Statement

    How can an integrated AI system automatically recognise and organise a person's existing clothes, recommend explainable outfits suited to their events, style and tropical micro-climate, and let them preview those outfits on a personalized animated avatar, so that wardrobe use becomes easier and more sustainable?

    Core Objectives

    MAIN OBJECTIVEDevelop an AI-powered smart wardrobe that automatically identifies and organizes clothing, recommends context-aware outfits from the user's own wardrobe and visualizes them on an animated 3D avatar, promoting sustainable clothing use.

    1. Automate clothing classification

      Build background removal and a CNN multi-attribute classifier (type, colour, pattern, fabric) linked to the wardrobe database and Google Trends analysis.

    2. Organize the wardrobe intelligently

      Implement wear count tracking, laundry logic, smart search indexing, wardrobe analytics and a sustainability rotation algorithm.

    3. Recommend explainable outfits

      Construct a Fashion Knowledge Graph with a dual-layer event and persona engine, micro-climate fabric logic and a Stable Diffusion inspiration studio.

    4. Visualize outfits on a 3D avatar

      Create a personalized avatar from a selfie and measurements, attach 3D clothing and animate it in AR with Unity and Mixamo.

    Methodology

    The system runs in four layers (data acquisition, AI, wardrobe intelligence and visualization), connected through backend APIs and a shared wardrobe database. MongoDB stores items and preferences; Neo4j stores the knowledge graph of items, colours, fabrics, events and style rules.

    1Capture

    User uploads garment photos, profile, selfie and optional body measurements.

    2Clean & Prepare

    U²-Net / Mask R-CNN removes background; images are resized to 224×224, normalized, de-noised and colour-corrected.

    3Classify

    CNN (MobileNet / ResNet-50) extracts features and predicts type, colour, pattern and fabric.

    4Organize

    Metadata, wear count, last-worn date and laundry status are stored and indexed.

    5Recommend

    Knowledge graph and context engine rank outfits and generate explanations.

    6Visualize

    The chosen outfit is rendered on the animated 3D avatar in AR.
